The Role

A digital forensics analyst collects and examines digital evidence from computers, phones, memory, network traffic and media files. They work out what happened, when and who was involved, keep the evidence safe and unchanged, and write reports that can stand up in court or in a company investigation.

terminal

Required Skills

Good knowledge of file systems, file signatures and metadata, Windows and Linux artefacts, and networking. Tools like Autopsy, FTK Imager, Volatility and Wireshark. On top of that, proper evidence handling (hashing, chain of custody), a careful eye for detail and clear report writing.

memory

Modern Relevance

Almost every crime now leaves a digital trail, from online scams to fraud and data theft. On top of that, AI-generated images and videos are getting harder to tell apart from real ones, so investigators need ways to check if media evidence is genuine. With the Cyber Security Act 2024 requiring critical sectors to report cyber incidents, the need for proper investigation will only grow.
